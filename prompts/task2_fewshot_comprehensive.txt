sentiments, positive and negative, according to the sentiment
list and the definitions below. The reviews are from the IMDB
Movie Review Dataset, a collection of informal, user-written

Sentiment List
1 - Positive
2 - Negative

Sentiment Definition

Positive Definition: Reviews that recommend the film or
evaluate it favorably overall — praise for the acting,
writing, direction, atmosphere, or emotional impact, reports
of having enjoyed or rewatched it, or a defence of the film
against its critics, even when the reviewer also names flaws.

Negative Definition: Reviews that dismiss the film or evaluate
it unfavorably overall — criticism of the acting, writing,
pacing, or coherence, reports of boredom, disappointment, or
regret over the time spent, or warnings to other viewers, even
when the reviewer also names something they liked.

Background of the IMDB Movie Review Dataset
The dataset was assembled by Maas et al. (2011) from reviews
posted publicly to the Internet Movie Database, and has since
become a standard benchmark for binary sentiment
classification. It holds 50,000 labeled reviews, split evenly
into a 25,000-review training half and a 25,000-review test
half, and balanced so that each half contains as many positive
as negative reviews. The labels were not annotated by hand:
they were derived from the star rating out of ten that the
reviewer attached to their own text, with a rating of 7 or
higher counted as positive and 4 or lower counted as negative.
Reviews rated 5 or 6 were discarded as neutral, so the
collection deliberately contains only strongly polarized
opinions — there are no genuinely lukewarm verdicts in it, and
a review that reads as balanced still resolves to a clear
recommendation one way or the other. No more than 30 reviews
were taken from any single film, so that the collection is not
dominated by a handful of widely reviewed titles.
Because the reviews are written by viewers rather than by
professional critics, they are informal and uneven in length,
running from a single sentence to several hundred words. They
carry uncorrected spelling and typing errors, capitalisation
used for emphasis, and <br /> artifacts left over from the
original HTML. A review often spends most of its length
summarising the plot, describing a tragic, violent, or
frightening story, before the reviewer states what they
thought of the film itself; it may compare the film to a
source novel, to an earlier adaptation, or to other work by
the same director; it may quote the reviewer's own star rating
in the text, as in "2/10" or "8 out of 10"; and it may argue
with other reviewers or with professional critics rather than
discuss the film directly. Sarcasm, mock praise, and
overstated enthusiasm are common, and carry the opposite of
their literal wording.
